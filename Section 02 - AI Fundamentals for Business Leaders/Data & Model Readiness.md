Data & model readiness (key points): Supervised learning needs paired input/answer examples, so an AI use case is only viable if the business already recorded the outcome it wants predicted — 10 years of loan repayments can train a scoring model, a failure nobody ever logged cannot, and you can't buy a history that was never captured. Because a model learns patterns from past data, it assumes the future resembles the past; when pricing, processes, or markets change, it keeps returning confident answers about a business that no longer exists, and no alert fires because nothing technically broke. This is drift: live data moves away from training data, accuracy degrades over time, and the model never learns from the requests it serves — so AI is an ongoing commitment requiring continuous monitoring and human oversight from launch day, not a project that ends at deployment. Bias is a related risk: imbalance in the training data or in prediction behavior across groups (age, income, etc.) means a model trained mostly on middle-aged customers is less accurate for younger and older ones, while high overall accuracy hides the gap because the affected group is small. Detecting both drift and bias requires baselines recorded before launch — normal input data, normal model outputs, and training-time bias metrics — which live values are then compared against; with no baseline, drift still happens but nobody can prove it.
