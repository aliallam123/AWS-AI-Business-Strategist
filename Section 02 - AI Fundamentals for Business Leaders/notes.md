Section 2: AI Fundamentals for business leaders
Data types & quality (key points): Structured data has a fixed shape — a schema defining each field and its type — and covers things like sales figures and inventory records; unstructured data has no fixed shape and covers documents, images, audio, and anything that never fitted a form. Most of what a company holds, and most of the new volume it creates each year, is unstructured, even though teams instinctively open the database first when planning an AI project. The shape of the data determines which kind of AI fits, so assess it before choosing a service: structured rows of past outcomes suit classic ML prediction (churn, unpaid invoices, predictive maintenance), while unstructured content is what generative AI opened up (reading a contract, extracting fields, summarising) — work that previously required a person reading every page. On quality: a model only knows what its data showed it, so it cannot correct bad records — it repeats them faster and at greater scale, and a wrong answer built on bad data looks identical to a right one, so people act on it. Named quality failures — missing values, inaccurate or irrelevant records, duplicates — are all fixable once someone looks; a coverage gap is not, because every record is technically correct, yet if a customer group is barely represented the model learns almost nothing about them and is least accurate for exactly that group. Data quality is also not a one-time cleanup: live systems ingest uncurated real-world data, so checks must continue long after launch.
